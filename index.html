About me

I am a researcher and academic affiliated with De Montfort University in Leicester, UK. My research focuses on neurodegeneration and the use of yeast cell models to investigate protein misfolding and cell death associated with conditions such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease.

My published research has explored compounds and mechanisms including radicicol, aegeline, nonantioxidant tetramethoxystilbene, and inhibitors of the Aβ42-induced endoplasmic reticular unfolded protein response. I have also co-authored research investigating the effects of acute and chronic methylphenidate exposure on neurochemistry and electrophysiology in the prefrontal cortex and striatum of rats.

Alongside my research, my academic interests include pharmacy education and teaching. My profile is also associated with the Leicester School of Pharmacy at De Montfort University and teaching and module materials in introductory biochemistry.

2022Neuropharmacology

Methylphenidate and brain neurotransmission

Investigating acute and longer-term effects of methylphenidate on dopamine and norepinephrine signalling in the prefrontal cortex and striatum.

ADHD medicationDopamineElectrophysiology
Explore study

Study overview
Rat-based ex vivo neurotransmitter assays and in vivo electrophysiology were used to examine how acute and chronic methylphenidate exposure affects neurotransmitter release and neuronal activity.

Key findings
Acute exposure promoted dopamine and norepinephrine efflux in the prefrontal cortex and dopamine efflux in the striatum. Following chronic exposure and a washout period, changes in prefrontal neuronal firing and striatal dopamine responses were still observed.

Research significance
The findings indicate that methylphenidate can produce lasting changes in neurotransmission in these brain regions; further work is needed to establish the clinical implications.

2019Natural products · Cell biology

Aegeline and protein-associated cell toxicity

Examining whether a natural product from Aegle marmelos can counter α-synuclein- and Bax-associated toxicity in a yeast model.

α-SynucleinApoptosisNatural products
Explore study

Study overview
A natural-product library was screened using yeast cells expressing human α-synuclein or Bax. Aegeline was identified as the only tested molecule reported to overcome apoptosis induced by both proteins in the yeast assays.

Key findings
Aegeline also countered growth inhibition in cells expressing the more toxic A53T α-synuclein mutant. The reported effects were associated with reduced reactive oxygen species, preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential and less nuclear DNA fragmentation.

Research significance
The work illustrates the use of yeast-cell models to identify natural compounds for further investigation in pathways relevant to Parkinson’s disease-associated protein toxicity.

2018Molecular and cellular research

Tetramethoxystilbene and α-synuclein toxicity

Screening methoxy-stilbenes to identify compounds that protect yeast cells from α-synuclein-associated growth inhibition and cell death.

Parkinson’s diseaseProtein misfoldingYeast model
Explore study

Study overview
A library of methoxy-stilbenes was screened in yeast models expressing human α-synuclein. Tetramethoxy-stilbene 4s was identified as a strong hit, including in models expressing wild-type and A53T-mutant α-synuclein.

Key findings
Compound 4s reduced indicators associated with cell injury, including loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, reactive oxygen species and nuclear DNA fragmentation. It did not rescue cells from Bax- or βA4-induced toxicity, suggesting a more selective effect in these yeast assays.

Research significance
The study supports the use of parallel yeast-cell screens to distinguish compounds that act on α-synuclein-associated toxicity from those affecting other cell-death pathways.

    Research articleACS Omega

    Nonantioxidant Tetramethoxystilbene Abrogates α-Synuclein-Induced Yeast Cell Death but Not That Triggered by the Bax or βA4 Peptide

    Read abstract & research details

    Abstract summary
    A library of methoxy-stilbenes was screened in yeast models expressing human α-synuclein. Tetramethoxy-stilbene 4s restored growth in cells expressing wild-type and A53T-mutant α-synuclein and reduced several apoptosis-associated changes, including reactive oxygen species, mitochondrial membrane-potential loss and DNA fragmentation. It did not rescue Bax- or βA4-induced cell death.

    Aegeline, a natural product from the plant Aegle marmelos, mimics the yeast SNARE protein Sec22p in suppressing α-synuclein and Bax toxicity in yeast

Screening identified aegeline, a natural product from Aegle marmelos, as a compound that countered α-synuclein- and Bax-associated toxicity in yeast, including toxicity linked to the A53T α-synuclein mutant.   3. 2019
    Research articleEuropean Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences

    Inhibitors of Aβ42-induced endoplasmic reticular unfolded protein response (UPRER), in yeast, also rescue yeast cells from Aβ42-mediated apoptosis

    Read abstract & research details

    Abstract summary
    The paper investigated the endoplasmic-reticulum unfolded protein response (UPRER) and apoptosis in yeast expressing human Aβ42. The reported model showed that secreted human Aβ42, unlike the mouse/rat peptide, induced UPRER and inhibited yeast-cell growth, with apoptosis-associated changes including mitochondrial membrane-potential loss, increased reactive oxygen species and DNA fragmentation. The study then examined UPRER inhibitors as a way to rescue affected yeast cells.

    Research articleBioorganic Chemistry

    Radicicol rescues yeast cell death triggered by expression of human α-synuclein and its A53T mutant, but not by human βA4 peptide and proapoptotic protein bax

    Read abstract & research details

    Abstract summary
    This study tested radicicol, an Hsp90 inhibitor isolated from a fungus, in yeast models expressing human α-synuclein, its A53T mutant, βA4 or Bax. Radicicol rescued cells from wild-type and mutant α-synuclein-associated toxicity, while reducing reactive oxygen species, reversing mitochondrial dysfunction and preventing nuclear DNA fragmentation. It did not rescue cells from βA4- or Bax-induced toxicity.
